What You Can Do¶

ClinVar-GKM turns each ClinVar release into typed records you can query directly: variants with computed identifiers, every submission (SCV) as a structured statement, and ClinVar's own aggregate calls (VCV, RCV) with the evidence lines that link them to the submissions behind them. This page is a gallery of questions that structure makes practical. Each card names the data it reads and links to the query, recipe or page that answers it.

Each card carries a status tag, the same scheme as the What It Unlocks presentation:

Tag Meaning
Available now The published data answers it today, with the query or page linked from the card.
Within reach The data supports it; the tool or app that packages it does not exist yet.
On the roadmap Needs data or features ClinVar-GKM does not publish yet.

Five of the cards have a full, tested query on this page, under In depth. The others link to the page that already covers them.

Explore a variant¶

  • What does ClinVar say about my variant?

    Available now · anyone with a variant

    Data: variation, the proposition tables, vcv, rcv, scv, submitter, condition

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: a variant's aggregate calls, submissions and conditions are spread over nested elements of one large record per accession.

    Try it: Everything ClinVar says about one variant · Concept: VCV Statements

  • Is my variant the one ClinVar classified?

    Available now · labs, knowledgebases, anyone joining ClinVar to their own data

    Data: allele (id, a ga4gh:VA.… VRS identifier), variation.members

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: matching means normalizing HGVS, transcripts and coordinates on both sides; with VRS the join is an equality on a computed id.

    Scope: each variant has one member allele, its defining allele (GRCh38 where ClinVar has one). An id computed on another assembly won't match, and no lifted-over allele is published to join on.

    Try it: User Story: Harmonizing ClinVar by VRS Identity · Concept: Variations

  • Can I recompute a variant's VRS identifier myself?

    Available now · developers, anyone checking identifiers

    Data: allele, location, sequenceReference, or the bundle

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: ClinVar's XML carries no VRS identifiers; computing one needs a normalized allele, which the bundle already holds.

    Try it: VRS alleles and their identifiers · Concept: GA4GH Standards

  • Which other identifiers point to this variant?

    Available now · integrators, curators

    Data: variation.mappings (ClinVar, dbSNP, ClinGen Allele Registry, and others), variation.extensions → clinvarHgvsList (MANE Select and MANE Plus Clinical flags, Sequence Ontology consequences)

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: cross-references and HGVS expressions sit in different parts of the record, each with its own shape.

    Try it: the variation columns · Concept: Mappings

  • Which papers back a variant's classifications?

    Available now · curators, researchers

    Data: scv.reportedIn (PubMed ids, DOIs, URLs), the proposition tables, variation

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: citations are attached at several levels of a submission, in mixed formats.

    Try it: PubMed ids cited for a variant · for every variant in a gene · Concept: Citations

Curate and audit¶

  • What would the call be under my own rules?

    Available now · curators, lab directors, methods researchers

    Data: scv, propositionPathogenicity, vcv

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: you would have to rebuild the submission-to-variant links and ClinVar's precedence rules before changing one of them.

    Try it: Re-run the aggregation (CLI; tested below) · Concept: VCV aggregation

  • Why is a variant conflicting, and who disagrees?

    Available now · curators, expert panels

    Data: vcv, evidenceLine, scv, submitter

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: the XML gives the conflict label and counts, not a table of which submission sits at which level and whether it counted.

    Try it: Why a variant is "conflicting" · Concept: Layer Hierarchy

  • How well were submitted conditions mapped?

    Available now · curators, ClinVar submitters, data stewards

    Data: scv.extensions → submittedCondition (normalized_resolution, original_medgen_match, direct_match), submitter

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: the XML gives the submitted trait and the RCV's trait, not how one was matched to the other.

    Try it: Measure condition-mapping quality (CLI; tested below) · Concept: SubmittedCondition

  • Where does a submitter's gene differ from the variant's gene?

    Available now · curators, submitters

    Data: the proposition tables' geneContextQualifier, with its submittedGeneSymbols and geneContextSource extensions

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: submitted gene symbols are free text beside the variant's gene list, with nothing saying whether they agree.

    Try it: Gene context (filter on geneContextSource = 'submittedSymbolDiffers') · Concept: Qualifier Extensions

  • What does our lab's ClinVar footprint look like?

    Available now · ClinVar submitters

    Data: submitter, scv, the proposition tables, condition, therapy

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: there is no export of "everything we submitted" as a table to re-run each release.

    Try it: User Story: Auditing a Submitter's ClinVar Footprint · One submitter's footprint · Concept: Review Status

  • Which of my variants were reclassified this week?

    Available now · labs re-contacting patients, curators watching a gene panel

    Data: a weekly delta and its manifest, your copy of last week's vcv table

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: each release is a new full file; finding what changed means diffing two of them yourself.

    Try it: A weekly reclassification alert (CLI; tested below) · Keep Your Data Current · Concept: The delta manifest

Oncology¶

  • Which variants predict response to a drug in a tumor type?

    Available now · molecular tumor boards, oncology knowledgebases

    Data: propositionSomaticClinicalImpact (therapeutic-response rows, conditionQualifier), therapy, therapyGroup, condition

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: drugs, drug combinations and the tumor context are free-text parts of a somatic submission, not linked records.

    Try it: Drug response in a tumor type (console or CLI; tested below; runs in the live console) · Concept: propositionSomaticClinicalImpact

  • Which somatic variants are Tier I or Tier II, and for what?

    Available now · oncology curators

    Data: evidenceLine (the SCV lines: targetProposition, evidenceOutcome), propositionSomaticClinicalImpact

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: the AMP/ASCO/CAP tier and the specific impact (diagnostic, prognostic, therapeutic) are separate pieces of a somatic submission.

    Try it: Reading a variant's statements and the evidenceLine columns · Concept: Proposition Types

Build and integrate¶

  • Can I drop my ClinVar XML parser?

    Available now · developers

    Data: a monthly full bundle or the Parquet set, then one weekly delta a week

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: a bespoke parser, variant normalization, and a patch for every XML change.

    Try it: Keep Your Data Current · Working in Python · Concept: Output Format

  • Can I get just my gene panel, as a small bundle or tables?

    Available now · labs, tool builders

    Data: any published bundle, cut with gkm-bundle-parquet select

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: a subset means parsing everything, then re-linking the pieces you kept.

    Try it: Cut a mini-bundle · Concept: ID References

  • Can I build a VCF scoped to my panel and quality bar?

    Within reach · clinical pipelines

    Data: allele (expressions: SPDI, gnomAD-style), vcv, the proposition tables

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: ClinVar's VCF is one fixed cut; a different one means starting from the XML.

    Status: the tables hold what such a VCF needs; no packaged exporter exists yet. A genomic window and the gene rollup show the joins.

  • Can my RAG system, agent or knowledge graph use ClinVar as ground truth?

    Within reach · AI and tool builders

    Data: the bundle: statements with explicit propositions (subject, predicate, object), typed classifications, stable ids

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: the same meaning is written in several shapes, so every consumer re-interprets it.

    Status: the data is in that shape today; the applications are for others to build. See Proposition Types.

  • Can I review and submit to ClinVar from my own system?

    Within reach · ClinVar submitters, LIMS vendors

    Data: your SCVs as VA-Spec statements (scv), compared with ClinVar's current calls (vcv)

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: comparing your records with ClinVar's means parsing ClinVar first.

    Status: comparison works today (User Story: Auditing a Submitter's ClinVar Footprint); submission tooling built on the model is an opportunity, not a product.

Research at scale¶

  • How have classifications changed since a past release?

    Available now, from 2026-06-27 · researchers, curators

    Data: the vcv table of today and of a past release, built from an archived monthly

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: two releases of XML in two shapes, each parsed and normalized, before any comparison.

    Try it: Compare classifications across releases (CLI; tested below) · Track Change Over Time · Concept: Previous Releases

  • How do ClinVar's calls roll up by gene?

    Available now · researchers, panel designers

    Data: variation (clinvarGeneList), gene, vcv

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: gene associations and the aggregate call are in different parts of each record.

    Try it: Gene rollup of ClinVar's calls · Concept: Gene context versus gene list

  • Which classifications carry a mode of inheritance or a penetrance qualifier?

    Available now · researchers, curators

    Data: propositionPathogenicity and propositionClinvarCustom: modeOfInheritanceQualifier (with its HPO code), penetranceQualifier

    Why it is hard with ClinVar XML: inheritance is free text on the submission, and low penetrance is folded into the classification text.

    Try it: The proposition columns · Concept: Qualifier Types

  • How has ClinVar changed over years, not months?

    On the roadmap · researchers

    Data: ClinVar-GKM's history starts with the 2026-06-27 release; earlier ClinVar releases are not published in this form.

    Status: comparison within the published range works today (above). Longer history, and reclassification trend studies across ClinVar's past, need earlier releases transformed. See the Roadmap.

  • Can I use case-level observations and functional evidence?

    On the roadmap · researchers, variant scientists

    Data: not yet in the bundle: ClinVar's case-level observations and functional data (including MaveDB) are planned.

    Status: see the Roadmap.

In depth¶

The five tested cards. Each query ran as written against the 2026-11-15 release (the Parquet set published 2026-11-20 at v1/1.2/parquet/); re-run them to get today's numbers.

Prerequisites

  • DuckDB CLI, tested with 1.5.4. No extension is needed for downloaded files; a query that names a https:// URL loads DuckDB's httpfs extension the first time.
  • The tables each query reads, downloaded into the directory you start duckdb from, as on Parquet Files → Download. Each section names its tables and their sizes. Drug response in a tumor type reads the published URLs and needs nothing downloaded.
  • A weekly reclassification alert and Compare classifications across releases also build tables from a bundle with gkm-bundle-parquet 2.0.0, run with uvx from uv (tested with 0.6.14), and use curl, python3 and shasum (macOS) or sha256sum (Linux). Each section's commands run in one directory, in bash or zsh: later blocks read the files earlier ones write.
  • The live console takes no query from a link. To run a query there, copy it into the console. Only Drug response in a tumor type reads tables small enough for it; the others read large tables, which the console stops after 15 seconds.

Re-run ClinVar's aggregation under your own rules¶

ClinVar's germline call for a variant comes from its highest-ranked submissions: practice guideline and expert panel above criteria provided, which is above no assertion criteria. Among submissions at the winning level, any disagreement between pathogenic or likely pathogenic, uncertain significance and benign or likely benign makes the call Conflicting. Every SCV, its level and its classification are rows, so you can change a rule and recompute the call for every variant.

The query below keeps only submissions with assertion criteria (it drops flagged and no assertion criteria submissions), lets the highest level win, and treats only P/LP against B/LB as a conflict: an uncertain significance submission no longer blocks a call. It then counts the variants whose call differs from ClinVar's. The lines marked -- <- are the rules; change them to try your own.

CLI. Tables: scv.parquet (785 MB), propositionPathogenicity.parquet (403 MB), vcv.parquet (148 MB). About 5 seconds on a laptop.

WITH sub AS (                    -- 1. the submissions your rule keeps
  SELECT p.subject AS variant,
         CASE WHEN s.quality.name IN ('practice guidelines', 'expert panel') THEN 2 ELSE 1 END AS rank,
         CASE WHEN lower(s.classification.primaryCoding.code) IN
                   ('pathogenic', 'likely pathogenic', 'pathogenic/likely pathogenic') THEN 'P/LP'
              WHEN lower(s.classification.primaryCoding.code) IN
                   ('benign', 'likely benign', 'benign/likely benign')                 THEN 'B/LB'
              WHEN lower(s.classification.primaryCoding.code) = 'uncertain significance'  THEN 'VUS'
         END AS tier
  FROM 'scv.parquet' s
  JOIN 'propositionPathogenicity.parquet' p ON p.id = s.proposition
  WHERE s.proposition_section = 'propositionPathogenicity'
    AND s.quality.name IN ('practice guidelines', 'expert panel', 'criteria provided')  -- <- drops flagged, no criteria
),
mine AS (                        -- 2. your call: the highest level wins; only P/LP against B/LB is a conflict
  SELECT variant,
         CASE WHEN bool_or(tier = 'P/LP') AND bool_or(tier = 'B/LB') THEN 'conflicting'
              WHEN bool_or(tier = 'P/LP') THEN 'P/LP'                 -- <- a VUS no longer blocks a P/LP call
              WHEN bool_or(tier = 'B/LB') THEN 'B/LB'
              WHEN bool_or(tier = 'VUS')  THEN 'VUS'
         END AS my_call
  FROM sub
  WHERE rank = (SELECT max(rank) FROM sub s2 WHERE s2.variant = sub.variant)
  GROUP BY variant
),
clinvar AS (                     -- 3. ClinVar's call: the germline pathogenicity root statement
  SELECT 'clinvar:' || CAST(substr(id, 4, 9) AS INTEGER) AS variant,
         CASE WHEN classification.name IN ('Pathogenic', 'Likely pathogenic', 'Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic') THEN 'P/LP'
              WHEN classification.name IN ('Benign', 'Likely benign', 'Benign/Likely benign')                 THEN 'B/LB'
              WHEN classification.name = 'Uncertain significance'                                             THEN 'VUS'
              WHEN classification.name LIKE 'Conflicting%'                                                    THEN 'conflicting'
         END AS clinvar_call
  FROM 'vcv.parquet'
  WHERE regexp_matches(id, '-G-PATH$')
)
SELECT clinvar_call, my_call, count(*) AS variants
FROM clinvar JOIN mine USING (variant)
WHERE clinvar_call IS DISTINCT FROM my_call
GROUP BY ALL
ORDER BY variants DESC;

clinvar_call  my_call  variants
conflicting   B/LB      152,833
conflicting   P/LP       29,763
NULL          VUS           191
NULL          P/LP           61
conflicting   VUS            58

Under this rule, 182,596 of ClinVar's 184,296 Conflicting variants get a single call: most of ClinVar's conflicts are a VUS against a benign or a pathogenic majority. The NULL rows are variants whose ClinVar call is outside the four groups (a risk allele, a low-penetrance call), and the conflicting → VUS rows conflict over such a call.

As a check, change rule 2 to ClinVar's own (WHEN count(DISTINCT tier) > 1 THEN 'conflicting'): the call then matches ClinVar's for all but 361 of the 4,323,971 variants the query covers, and each of those 361 has a submission at the winning level outside the four groups. The query covers only variants with at least one submission that has assertion criteria; ClinVar still calls the others (132,157 more) from their no assertion criteria submissions.

A research tool, not a new classification

The recomputed call is yours. ClinVar's precedence rules, and the reasons a submitter's VUS counts, are in Review Status and the VCV aggregation rules.

Measure condition-mapping quality¶

A submitter names a condition with a name, a MedGen id, or ids from OMIM, MONDO, HPO or Orphanet. ClinVar maps it to one of its conditions, and ClinVar-GKM records how, on each single-condition SCV, in the submittedCondition extension:

  • normalized_resolution: which reference matched (tm reftype xref medgen is the submitted MedGen id, tm reftype preferred name the submitted name, and so on). random trait assignment means none of them matched ClinVar's trait mapping, and the condition was paired with the RCV's one remaining unmatched condition.
  • original_medgen_match: set when the submitted MedGen concept was remapped to a different one.
  • direct_match: set when the condition the submitted reference matched directly differs from the final one.

CLI. Tables: scv.parquet (785 MB). About 10 seconds.

WITH sc AS (
  SELECT [e.value_object FOR e IN extensions IF e.name = 'submittedCondition'][1] AS c
  FROM 'scv.parquet'
)
SELECT coalesce(c.normalized_resolution, '(none)')    AS resolution,
       count(*)                                        AS scvs,
       count(c.original_medgen_match)                  AS medgen_remapped,
       count(c.direct_match)                           AS direct_differs
FROM sc
WHERE c IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY ALL
ORDER BY scvs DESC;

Where that happens most, by submitter (submitter.parquet, 138 KB, as well):

WITH sc AS (
  SELECT [c.contributor FOR c IN contributions IF c.activityType = 'submitted'][1]            AS submitter,
         [e.value_object.normalized_resolution FOR e IN extensions
          IF e.name = 'submittedCondition'][1]                                                AS resolution
  FROM 'scv.parquet'
)
SELECT sub.name                                                      AS submitter,
       count(*)                                                      AS scvs,
       count(*) FILTER (resolution = 'random trait assignment')      AS random_assignment,
       round(100.0 * random_assignment / scvs, 1)                    AS pct
FROM sc
JOIN 'submitter.parquet' sub ON sub.id = sc.submitter
WHERE resolution IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY ALL
HAVING scvs >= 1000                                                  -- <- only submitters with 1,000+ SCVs
ORDER BY pct DESC
LIMIT 10;

Drug response in a tumor type¶

A somatic clinical-impact submission can say that a variant predicts sensitivity or resistance to a drug, or to a combination of drugs given together, in a tumor type. Each such claim is a therapeutic-response proposition: its object is a therapy or a therapyGroup (a combination, membershipOperator AND), and its conditionQualifier is the tumor type.

Console or CLI. This query reads four small tables from the published URLs (about 2 MB together), so it runs in the live console (about 2 seconds) as well as the DuckDB CLI:

SELECT p.subject                                  AS variant,
       p.geneContextQualifier.name                AS gene,
       p.predicate,
       coalesce(t.name, (SELECT string_agg(m.name, ' + ' ORDER BY m.name)
                         FROM 'https://data.clinvar-gkm.org/v1/1.2/parquet/therapy.parquet' m
                         WHERE list_contains(g.concepts, m.id)))   AS therapy,
       tumor.name                                 AS tumor_type,
       count(*)                                   AS scvs
FROM 'https://data.clinvar-gkm.org/v1/1.2/parquet/propositionSomaticClinicalImpact.parquet' p
LEFT JOIN 'https://data.clinvar-gkm.org/v1/1.2/parquet/therapy.parquet'      t ON t.id = p.object
LEFT JOIN 'https://data.clinvar-gkm.org/v1/1.2/parquet/therapyGroup.parquet' g ON g.id = p.object
LEFT JOIN 'https://data.clinvar-gkm.org/v1/1.2/parquet/condition.parquet'    tumor ON tumor.id = p.conditionQualifier
WHERE p.type = 'VariantTherapeuticResponseProposition'
GROUP BY ALL
ORDER BY gene, therapy;

A weekly reclassification alert¶

A delta lists the records a week replaced, and holds their new versions. Since a new ClinVar version is a new key (VCV000066284.2-G-PATH becomes VCV000066284.3-G-PATH), a reclassification shows up as a key in the manifest's deleted list and a record with the same accession and statement code in the delta. Pair them, look the old key up in last week's vcv table, and compare the calls. A new version doesn't always mean a new call: most version bumps are a new or updated submission that leaves the call as it was.

CLI. You need:

  • Last week's vcv.parquet, in a folder last-week/: the published file you downloaded the week before, or the copy you keep current (Keep Your Data Current). For a week whose Parquet is no longer published, build it: Parquet for one weekly release.
  • This week's delta and its manifest, and the delta's vcv table. The commands look the paths up in the line's index.json (a delta moves to its month's folder when the month ends), then build the table, in about 10 seconds. They work in bash and zsh:
P=(--bundle-schema https://w3id.org/clingen/schema/clinvar-gkm/1.2.0/clinvar-gkm-bundle.schema.json --version-property schema_version)
curl -fsS -o index.json https://data.clinvar-gkm.org/v1/1.2/index.json
for f in $(python3 -c '
import json, sys
d = next(x for x in json.load(open("index.json"))["deltas"] if x["release"] == sys.argv[1])
print(d["bundle"]["path"], d["manifest"])' 2026-11-08); do
  curl -fsS -O "https://data.clinvar-gkm.org/$f"
done
uvx gkm-bundle-parquet@2.0.0 build --full clinvar-gkm-delta_2026-1108.json.gz "${P[@]}" --tables vcv --out delta/

Change 2026-11-08 (and the file name in the last line) to the week you are checking. Then, in duckdb, list the variants on your watch list whose call changed:

WITH watch(accession) AS (       -- <- your variants' VCV accessions
  VALUES ('VCV000000010'), ('VCV000051592'), ('VCV000066284'), ('VCV000546873')
),
replaced AS (                    -- keys the delta removed: last week's versions of the records it replaced
  SELECT unnest(sections.vcv.deleted) AS id
  FROM read_json('clinvar-gkm-delta_2026-1108.manifest.json', maximum_object_size = 200000000)
),
old AS (                         -- last week's root statements that were replaced
  SELECT split_part(id, '.', 1) AS accession, regexp_replace(id, '^[^-]+', '') AS statement,
         classification.name AS old_call
  FROM 'last-week/vcv.parquet'
  WHERE id IN (SELECT id FROM replaced)
    AND NOT regexp_matches(id, '-(PG|EP|CP|NOCP|NOCL|FLAG)(-T[0-9])?$')
),
new AS (                         -- this week's root statements, from the delta

Compare classifications across releases¶

ClinVar-GKM's history starts with the 2026-06-27 release: the oldest monthly, 2026-07 in the frozen v1/1.1/ line. Every release since then can be compared with today's, from a monthly, or a monthly plus the weekly deltas after it. Earlier ClinVar releases are not published in this form.

CLI. The published Parquet is only the current release. For a past one, build the table you need from its bundle. Here, only the germline pathogenicity root statements of the 2026-07 monthly, with gkm-bundle-parquet and the 1.1.0 column set (the monthly is in the 1.1 line):

M=$(curl -fsS https://data.clinvar-gkm.org/v1/1.1/index.json | python3 -c '
import json, sys
print(next(m["path"] for m in json.load(sys.stdin)["datasets"]["monthly"] if m["release"] == "2026-07"))')
curl -f --retry 5 -O "https://data.clinvar-gkm.org/$M" -O "https://data.clinvar-gkm.org/$M.sha256"
shasum -a 256 -c clinvar-gkm_2026-07.json.gz.sha256
uvx gkm-bundle-parquet@2.0.0 build --full clinvar-gkm_2026-07.json.gz \
    --bundle-schema https://w3id.org/clingen/schema/clinvar-gkm/1.1.0/clinvar-gkm-bundle.schema.json \
    --column-set https://dataexchange.clinicalgenome.org/clinvar-gkm/1.2/assets/column-sets/clinvar-gkm-1.1.0.columns.yaml \
    --driver vcv --where "regexp_matches(id, '-G-PATH$')" --max-depth 0 \
    --out vcv-2026-07/

The monthly is 4.5 GB. The build streams it and keeps only the selected rows: 11 minutes on a 16 GB laptop, writing vcv-2026-07/vcv.parquet (4,273,584 rows). On Linux, use sha256sum -c for shasum -a 256 -c. Then compare it with the current vcv.parquet (148 MB), matching on the VCV accession without its version:

WITH then_ AS (                  -- the 2026-06-27 release, built from the 2026-07 monthly
  SELECT split_part(id, '.', 1) AS accession, classification.name AS call_2026_06_27
  FROM 'vcv-2026-07/vcv.parquet'
  WHERE regexp_matches(id, '-G-PATH$')
),
now_ AS (                        -- the current Parquet set
  SELECT split_part(id, '.', 1) AS accession, classification.name AS call_now
  FROM 'vcv.parquet'
  WHERE regexp_matches(id, '-G-PATH$')
)
SELECT call_2026_06_27, call_now, count(*) AS variants
FROM then_ JOIN now_ USING (accession)
WHERE call_2026_06_27 <> call_now
GROUP BY ALL
ORDER BY variants DESC
LIMIT 10;

What this comparison can and can't tell you

  • The range is short. Under five months of releases, not ClinVar's history. Trend studies over years need earlier releases transformed, which is on the roadmap.
  • It compares two snapshots. To see each step in between, compare consecutive releases, or read the weekly deltas' manifests as in A weekly reclassification alert.
  • The two releases are from different lines. The 2026-07 monthly has schema 1.1.0, today's tables 1.2.0. The VCV ids and classification.name are the same in both; other columns can differ (Parquet for Previous Releases).
